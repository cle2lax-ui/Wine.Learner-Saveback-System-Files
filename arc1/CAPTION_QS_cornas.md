## CAPTION — paste as-is

Cornas is the only northern Rhône cru whose rules permit no white grape at all.

Every other appellation on the river allows one somewhere — Côte-Rôtie can co-ferment up to 20% Viognier into the red, Saint-Joseph and Crozes permit Marsanne and Roussanne, and Condrieu and Saint-Péray make nothing but white. Cornas is red, and only red, and only Syrah.

145 hectares in a south- and east-facing amphitheatre, warm and well sheltered from the cold winds. That aspect is why it is usually the first Syrah picked in the north, and why the wines built a reputation for tannin you could stand a spoon in.

Two bottles that show the argument the appellation is still having. Clape is the traditional house — whole-bunch, old vines, high tannin, full body, and a wine that will outlive most of its buyers. Vincent Paris destems, and Granit 30 is the approachable face of the same slopes. The odd part: Clape is the more structured wine at the lower alcohol.

Tomorrow: the full Field Guide to the northern Rhône. Seven crus, one grape, twelve slides.
