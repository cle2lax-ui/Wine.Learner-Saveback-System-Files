## CAPTION — paste as-is

Northern Rhône cru reds are made both ways, and the choice changes the wine more than the vintage does.

Stems in: the bunches go into the fermenter intact, stalks and all. You get aeration of the must, plus perfume, freshness and fine tannin. Stems out: destemmed, chilled, cold soaked one to three days to draw out colour, and nothing green can reach the wine.

It turns on ripeness, not on style and not on tradition. Ripe stems give. Unripe stems take — green astringent tannin, and lower acidity, which is the last thing you want in a warm vintage.

Our position: whole bunch, in the years that earn it. Syrah's aromatics are the point of the northern Rhône and stems amplify them.

And yet Burgundy destemmed almost completely after Jayer, then came back to whole bunch decades later. Which of those was the fashion?

Where do you land — and does your answer change in a hot year?
