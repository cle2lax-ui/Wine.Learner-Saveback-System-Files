## CAPTION — paste as-is

The most famous appellation in the northern Rhône is 137 hectares, and a third of it is white.

Five things about the hill:

It is named after a man who quit. Gaspard de Stérimberg, a twelfth-century crusader who became a hermit — though the hill had been producing wine since the Greco-Roman era, so the name is the recent part.

The best Syrah sites are the hottest ones, at the western end of the hill, Le Méal among them.

In very ripe years it makes a rare sweet wine, Vin de Paille, from grapes dried off the vine.

And two opposed philosophies own it. Chapoutier pioneered working individual parcels. Chave has spent a career arguing for blending across the whole appellation. Between them sits the co-operative Cave de Tain, which owns 15 per cent of the hill outright.

**Hook** is 92 characters. The white-wine proportion is the fact most likely to surprise someone who thinks they know Hermitage, which is exactly what an FFFA hook is for.
