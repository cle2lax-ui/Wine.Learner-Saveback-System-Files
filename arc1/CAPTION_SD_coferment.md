# SAT 19 SEP · SPLIT DECISION · Viognier in Côte-Rôtie
"Co-ferment, or Don't" · 2 pages · Arc 1, week two

## CAPTION — paste as-is

One appellation in France lets you put a white grape into a great red wine. Almost nobody takes the offer.

Côte-Rôtie permits up to 20 per cent Viognier alongside Syrah. Where both are used they must be co-fermented, not blended afterwards — a different operation producing something blending cannot. In practice the proportion is often zero, and normally no more than 8 per cent.

FOR: Viognier adds floral and fruity aromas, and Côte-Rôtie's whole reputation rests on pronounced aroma. The practice is native here, from the era when the two grapes were interplanted.

AGAINST: Côte-Rôtie is already the softest and least full-bodied of the top northern Rhône appellations. A white grape softens it further. And the practice belongs to a cooler era — the fruit arrives fully ripe now, off high sunlight interception, rapid drainage and poor stony soils.

The ceiling is 20 per cent. Real use is usually under 8. So the argument was settled in practice long before anyone settled it in principle — which leaves a better question than the one I asked. If almost nobody uses the allowance, should it still be there?
