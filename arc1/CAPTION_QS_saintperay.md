## CAPTION — paste as-is

Two neighbouring crus, each of which has legislated the other's entire production out of existence.

Cornas permits one grape, Syrah, and no white wine may be made there at all. Cross south into Saint-Péray — the most southerly appellation in the northern Rhône — and the position inverts exactly. White only. Marsanne for the vast majority of plantings, with some Roussanne.

The ground explains part of it: limestone and granitic soils that hold water and drain at the same time, in a spot slightly cooler than its immediate neighbours. Maximum yield 45 hL/ha against Cornas's 40. The better wines age ten to twelve months on the lees, and some producers stir them for body.

And it makes sparkling wine. Traditional method, same two varieties — and, per the books, increasingly rare. A traditional-method sparkler inside a cru appellation, quietly going extinct.
