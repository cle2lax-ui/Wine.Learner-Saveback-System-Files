## CAPTION — paste as-is

Three and a half hectares built the reputation of an entire grape variety.

That's Château-Grillet — a single estate on the right bank of the northern Rhône that is also its own appellation, domaine-bottling since the 1830s, enclosed on all sides by Condrieu. It established Viognier as one of France's great white wines long before the variety came back into fashion in the 1980s.

Five facts on the swipe. Why the picking window is unforgiving in both directions. Why it buds early, sets badly, and gets grown on poles. Why Condrieu is the world's reference point for the grape at 197 hectares and 41 hL/ha. And why a white grape ends up co-fermented into a red.

The one most people get wrong is the last one — Viognier goes into Côte-Rôtie, up to 20 per cent, and it has to be co-fermented with the Syrah rather than blended in afterwards. Fermented together, or it doesn't count.
