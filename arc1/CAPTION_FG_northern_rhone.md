## CAPTION — paste as-is

In 1971 Côte-Rôtie was down to about 70 hectares and close to being given up on entirely.

The northern Rhône is six per cent of the Rhône's AOC production. The southern half — the one most people picture when they hear "Rhône" — is essentially all of the rest: 65,000 hectares of Grenache-led blends against 4,200 hectares of a single black grape.

Twelve slides on how those 4,200 hectares divide. The seven crus north to south, why the Mistral makes better wine and worse business, the 20% Viognier rule almost nobody uses, and why Syrah gets barrels while Grenache gets concrete.

The map on slide two is drawn from the INAO delimited parcellaire rather than commune outlines, so the shapes are the land actually permitted to each appellation. Worth reading them as permission, not planting — Crozes-Hermitage is delimited across roughly twice what is under vine.

Six of the seven crus are red, and every one of them is Syrah. Everything that varies here is site, not variety.
