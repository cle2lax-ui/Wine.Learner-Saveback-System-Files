# The Fifth Pass — "Taylor Swift's Social Team"

An addendum to `DESIGN_PROCESS_v8.md` §5. Runs **after** Wintour, Chanel,
Ive and Vignelli, never before. The first four passes decide whether the
deck is any good. This one decides whether anyone will pass it on.

Order matters and is not negotiable. A deck that fails Wintour cannot be
rescued by this pass, and this pass must never be allowed to argue a claim
back onto a slide that Wintour cut. Accuracy outranks engagement, always.

---

## What is actually being borrowed

Swift's team is not primarily good at "posting." They are good at making
the audience do work, and at rewarding that work publicly. Three
mechanisms recur across the analyses, and all three transfer to an
education-led wine account:

**The decode layer.** Deliberately planted details that reward close
reading — hidden references embedded across posts, videos and even
wardrobe, which fans hunt down and debate publicly. The engagement is
generated by the audience talking to *each other*, not to the account.

**Co-creation.** The strategy converts passive audiences into active
participants, which produces a continuous stream of earned attention
without a single announcement. Fans feel like collaborators rather than
consumers.

**Predictable unpredictability.** The two unannounced songs per show are
the cleanest example: a fixed ritual with a variable payload, so the
audience arrives already primed to guess, and posts their guess before
they get the answer.

**What does not transfer.** Scarcity drops, era-reveal choreography and
merch variants all depend on a parasocial fandom that a wine education
account does not have and should not pretend to. Do not import them. The
mechanics above work because they respect the audience's intelligence,
which is exactly the register this account already holds.

A caveat worth keeping in view: almost all of the source material on
these tactics is marketing commentary written after the fact, not
disclosure from the team itself. Treat the mechanisms as sound and the
causal claims as unproven.

## The eight gates

Every deck must pass all eight before it ships. Answer each with a
specific slide number, not "yes."

**1. The Decode Layer — one planted detail per deck.**
One element that a CSW or Diploma candidate will notice and a casual
reader will not, never called out in the copy. A vintage on a label in a
photo. A vineyard visible in the background of an atlas inset. A term
used with deliberate precision where the common usage is wrong. It must
be genuinely there and genuinely findable — a fake easter egg is worse
than none, because the audience that would find it is the audience that
will catch you.

*Which slide, and what is planted?*

**2. The Save Asset — one slide that is a tool, not a story.**
A reference the reader will need again later and cannot easily assemble
themselves. A classification table, a commune-by-commune roll call, a
comparison grid. Saves are the strongest signal available to a deck
format, and they only happen when the slide has future utility. This slide
should be the densest in the deck and is the one exemption from the 105-
word body budget.

*Which slide is the save asset?*

**3. The Send Line — one sentence built to be forwarded.**
A single counterintuitive fact, stated in under twenty words, that a
student would DM to their study group. Private sharing is reported to
carry three to five times the weight of a like for reaching people who
don't already follow. This sentence should be the most quotable line in
the deck and should sit alone on its slide, not buried in a paragraph.

*Which slide, and what is the line?*

**4. The Open Question — one answerable disagreement.**
Placed on the final slide. Not "what do you think?" but a question with
real competing answers that a knowledgeable person can defend: a
promotion dispute, a stylistic split, a classification anomaly. The
audience must be able to demonstrate expertise by answering. That is what
makes them answer.

*What is the question, and what are the two defensible positions?*

**5. The Callback — one link to a previous deck.**
An explicit reference to something covered before, phrased so a new
reader is intrigued rather than excluded. This is what turns a single post
into an archive worth scrolling, and it is the closest honest analogue to
Swift's cross-era continuity.

*Which previous deck, and where is the callback?*

**6. The Cover Hook — slide one must cost something to ignore.**
A cover that names the topic is a table of contents. The cover has to
open a loop the reader needs the second slide to close. This is the gate
the FFFA cover template currently fails, and the one most likely to
require a locked-template exemption.

*What loop does the cover open?*

**7. The Search Surface — caption written for a search engine.**
Instagram Search now reads caption language, and hashtags were capped at
five per post in December 2025 and confirmed not to improve reach. The
caption must contain the exact phrases a student would type: appellation
names, grape names, soil terms, "CSW," "WSET." Written as prose, not as a
keyword dump.

*Which terms are in the caption?*

**8. The Second Beat — the deck is not finished when it posts.**
One planned follow-up: the answer to the open question, the decode-layer
reveal, a Story poll, a reply that features a follower's answer. Two-way
conversation is itself a ranked signal now, so this gate pays twice.

*What is the second beat, and when does it run?*

## Failure modes to watch

- **Engagement bait dressed as a question.** Gate 4 fails if the question
  has no wrong answer. "Which do you prefer?" is bait. "Should Les
  Saint-Georges be promoted?" is a question.
- **The decode layer becoming the point.** If the deck is unreadable
  without spotting the easter egg, gate 1 has eaten the deck.
- **Register drift.** Every gate here can be satisfied without a single
  exclamation mark, listicle or "you won't believe." If satisfying a gate
  requires dropping the luxury-authority voice, the gate is being
  satisfied wrong.
- **Retrofitting.** These gates shape the outline, not the final render.
  Running this pass for the first time at review stage means rebuilding.
