# Split Decision — Style Guide v1

Two-slide comparative format, new in v9. Built to generate argument,
not to settle one.

## Purpose

Every other series in the system resolves. Field Guide teaches, FFFA
delivers, Quick Sips summarises, GTR has a right answer. Split Decision
is the only format designed to leave the reader with something to say,
because comment depth and sends-per-reach are the signals that move
reach beyond the existing follower graph.

## Structure — locked at two slides

**Slide 1 — The question.**
- Kicker: `SPLIT DECISION`
- Headline: the either/or, stated as a real question, mixed-case serif
- Two labelled poles, set as opposing columns (`duel`, `mode="columns"`)
- No answer, no hint, no hedge. The slide must be genuinely readable as
  a question a knowledgeable person could answer either way.

**Slide 2 — The mechanism, then the handoff.**
- The technical substance: why the two differ, sourced to D1/D3
- A stated position — the deck takes a side, briefly
- A closing line that re-opens it: the case for the other side, or the
  condition under which the answer flips
- Footer label suppressed (no SWIPE arrow; it is the final slide)

## The question test

A Split Decision question must fail all three of these to ship:

1. **Is there a settled answer?** If D3 states one plainly, it is a
   Quick Sips fact, not a Split Decision.
2. **Is it a matter of pure taste?** "Which do you prefer" generates
   shallow comments and teaches nothing.
3. **Is it unanswerable without tasting?** The reader must be able to
   reason from mechanism, not just from memory of a bottle.

Good: Côte Blonde or Côte Brune for longevity. Whole-bunch or destem in
Syrah. Chaptalisation or acidification in a warming Mosel. Parcel
selection or appellation blending in Hermitage.

Bad: best Bordeaux vintage. Favourite Champagne house. Screwcap versus
cork (settled enough to be a Field Guide slide, not an argument).

## Palette

Inherits the arc's Field Guide palette so the pair read as siblings on
the grid. The two poles must be visually differentiated by `MARK`
colour, not by size or weight — neither side may look like the
favoured one before the reader has read it.

## Copy budget

Slide 1: headline plus two column descriptions, 25 words maximum each.
Slide 2: 105-word standard slide budget, unchanged.

Cut copy rather than shrink type. Standard rule, no exemption here.

## Caption

The caption is doing more work in this format than in any other. The
close is always a genuine open question, and it must not be the same
question the slides already asked — it should push one level deeper,
into the condition or the edge case.
