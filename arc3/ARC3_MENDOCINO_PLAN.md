## Facts, by source (verified so far)
**D3 Ch. 23.1 (default):**
- Mendocino County: about 1 million ha, of which about 7,000 ha are under vine.
- Two halves: cooler AVAs near the Pacific (Anderson Valley: Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, aromatic whites) and warmer inland AVAs
  (Redwood Valley: Zinfandel, Syrah, Petite Sirah, Cabernet Sauvignon), though high inland sites such as Potter Valley produce
  Sauvignon Blanc and Riesling.
- Mendocino grapes are generally priced below Napa and Sonoma and often go into multi-regional blends.
- The Mendocino AVA (from the Russian River headwaters) covers six AVAs: Anderson Valley, Yorkville Highlands, McDowell Valley,
  Potter Valley, Redwood Valley, Cole Ranch.
- Anderson Valley: runs north-west to south-east, a few miles from the Pacific at its north-west end; cold air and fog are funnelled
  inland along the Navarro River (cold evenings and mornings); it narrows and warms inland; 900-2,000 mm of rain a year, wettest in
  the north-west; most vineyards on the valley's slopes; spring frost in low spots; just under 1,000 ha planted.
- Anderson Valley wines: Pinot Noir and Chardonnay lead, for sparkling and still; the Pinot Noir is fresh raspberry, cherry and plum,
  medium body, medium (+) acidity, generally good to outstanding and premium-priced; Alsace-style whites (Gewurztraminer, Riesling,
  Pinot Gris, Pinot Blanc); about 30 wineries; wineries elsewhere in California buy its fruit for Anderson Valley bottlings.
**Other authoritative sources (where D3 is silent or out of date):**
- AVAs: **12 wholly within the county, plus Pine Mountain-Cloverdale Peak shared with Sonoma** (TTB list, current). D3's "12"
  predates **Comptche (approved April 2024)**, which the TTB **specifically excluded from the North Coast AVA** around it: its climate
  and soils differ.
- **Mendocino Ridge**, "Islands in the Sky": the only noncontiguous AVA in the US, defined by elevation (1,200 ft / 366 m and above,
  above the fog line); about 250 acres planted out of roughly 250,000; new plantings mostly Pinot Noir, plus old-vine Zinfandel
  (CSW Study Guide; Mendocino trade fact sheet).
- **Cole Ranch:** the smallest AVA in the US; about 150-189 acres in total (sources differ), roughly 55-60 acres of vines; established
  1983. **D3 is wrong here** ("a couple of hundred hectares"); the CSW Study Guide's "60 acres" is the planted area. The single-owner
  detail is not safe to print (the whole AVA was listed for sale in 2019).
- **Organic:** 25% of Mendocino's vineyards certified organic, the highest share in the US (CSW Study Guide, 2018). **To re-check
  against a current figure before it ships.**

## Style evolution: bringing the decks into the Reel's language
What made the Reel work, and what each format would adopt:
1. **Dark grounds with flag-color bands** as dividers and transitions (the theme's colors: a country's flag, or burgundy
   (104, 26, 38) / forest green (21, 63, 40) for general topics: both are the series' own, from the Cote de Nuits and Saint-Peray decks).
2. **Condensed black capitals for headlines** (Archivo Condensed Black), with **Playfair italic** for the human line.
3. **A chip naming the series** on every page (FIELD GUIDE, GUESS THE REGION, WHAT AM I DRINKING?, FIVE FASCINATING FACTS).
4. **One big number per page where there is one** (70%, 61%, 32%): the stat as the hero.
5. **Data as illustration** (the ladder, the ring, the bars) rather than paragraphs.
6. **Photos darkened under type with gradients**, and **cream paper** for product and bottle pages.
Locked decks stay as they are unless Steve asks. The first step is a set of mockups (one page per format, US theme) for approval
before any format is converted.
