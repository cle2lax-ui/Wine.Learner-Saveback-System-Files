# Guess the Region: Anderson Valley (Arc 3, entry 2) -- Reel plan

**Format:** a Reel (about 40 s), theme `usa`. It keeps the series' Guess the Region DNA (GTR_STYLE_GUIDE; the Baden post):
four clues, A to D, each checkable; the answer never named before the reveal; one clue a "release valve" that makes it
solvable; facts deliberately not used, documented. New for the Reel: the clues ESCALATE (hardest first, the release valve
last) and a points meter drops with each clue (4 if you get it on clue A, 1 on clue D), so viewers commit early.
It tests The Field Guide, posted 2-3 days earlier: clue D is its callback.

## The clues
| Clue | Points | On screen | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (hardest) | 4 | "Its locals once invented their own lingo. In it, *bahl hornin'* means good drinking." | Boontling: an invented jargon of Boonville, late 1800s, heyday 1880-1920, about 1,600 words; "bahl hornin'" = good drinking (Anderson Valley Winegrowers; Atlas Obscura; Time; Wikipedia) |
| B | 3 | "A narrow valley a few miles from the Pacific, running north-west to south-east." | D3 Ch. 23.1 |
| C | 2 | "Cold air and fog funnel up a river into it, and most of its vineyards climb the slopes." | D3 Ch. 23.1 |
| D (release valve) | 1 | "Pinot Noir and Chardonnay, sparkling and still -- and Alsace's grapes, nine degrees south of Alsace." | D3 Ch. 23.1; geography; The Field Guide's own latitude scene |

**Reveal:** ANDERSON VALLEY -- Mendocino County, California. The real AVA lights up on the map (California -> county ->
valley, as in The Field Guide). Blurb: just under 1,000 ha planted; its Pinot Noir: fresh red fruit, medium (+) acidity
(D3). "Watched The Field Guide? Clue D was your way in."
**Close:** "How many points did you score? Tell us below." (a real open question: the answer is the viewer's own result).

## Deliberately NOT used
- The word "Boontling" (it contains "Boont", short for Boonville: an instant giveaway to anyone who knows it).
- The Navarro River, Boonville and Philo by name (each gives the answer away to a specialist before clue D).
- Roederer Estate (it is entry 3, What Am I Drinking?).
- "One of the coolest areas in California" (CSW): true, but it narrows nothing on its own.

**Visuals per clue (none gives the answer away):** A, kinetic type and a speech-bubble illustration of the phrase; B, an
illustrated valley between hills, running NW-SE, no labels; C, the Mendocino redwoods-in-fog photo (Pexels; no location
claimed) with fog drifting up a valley; D, a mini latitude ruler and rising bubbles (The Field Guide's own visuals,
deliberately). Reveal: the real map (UC Davis CC0), then the Anderson Valley hillside (Murayama, CC BY 2.0: credit in
the caption).

## v2 changes (Steve)
- Ident: the series' folded-map-and-pin logo, animated (the map starts open, spins a little, the pin drops in).
- Clue B: drawn from the AVA and county data, not stylized: the real coastline, the real valley polygon (unlabeled, no
  county lines), its true NW-SE axis, and the measured distance to the Pacific (about 4 miles).

## v3 changes (Steve)
- Clue A is now about viticulture, not the local dialect (Boontling is dropped entirely): "Its vineyards see about 35 to 79
  inches of rain a year, and spring frost threatens the low-lying spots." D3 Ch. 23.1 gives 900-2,000 mm; shown in inches
  at 25.4 mm to the inch (35.4 and 78.7, rounded). Still the hardest clue: many cool coastal regions are wet and have frost
  pockets. Illustrated with a rain gauge filling past 35 toward 79 inches, and a hillside cross-section where cold air
  drains into the hollow and frost settles there. Not used: the origin of the valley's name (said to be an early settler
  family), since neither D3 nor the CSW guide covers it.
- The countdown reads "MAKE YOUR GUESS, / THE ANSWER IS COMING IN" (the series' all-caps headline style), with a larger
  countdown below.

## v4 changes (Steve)
- No rules page: the ident goes straight to CLUE #1. No points anywhere: the meter and the difficulty labels are gone, the
  clues are CLUE #1 .. CLUE #4, and the close asks HOW MANY CLUES DID YOU NEED? (tiles #1-#4).
- The reveal holds one second longer (9 s); the cut into the close lands on a beat rather than a downbeat.
- The ident: the folded map unfolds (its four panels stacked accordion-style, opening continuously) while it spins; then
  the route draws and the pin drops.
- Running time 44 s.

## v5 changes (Steve)
- The ident runs 1.5x faster: 2 s instead of 3 (the timeline is now built from the scene lengths, so it followed). The
  sonic logo is rescaled to match; its bells ring on into clue #1 rather than being cut off.
- GUESS THE / REGION is a two-line wordmark chip, sized to the largest that fits the width (a one-line chip could only grow
  about 1.5x).
- The map pin is red, matching the wordmark chip, with a white inner ring.
- Running time 43 s.
