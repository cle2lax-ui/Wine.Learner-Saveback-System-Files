## CAPTION: paste as-is

Only about 4 percent of Anderson Valley is vines. Five more facts the Mendocino Field Guide left out.

A Champagne house founded Roederer Estate in Anderson Valley in 1982. Cold air settles on the valley floor, and most of the vineyards sit on the slopes. And one Mendocino AVA sits above the fog altogether: the 1997 federal rule that created it quotes the writer Matt Kramer saying its ridge sites "rarely see the spring frosts."

For WSET Diploma and CSW candidates, the last page is the one to hold on to. Philo, Boonville and Ukiah climb the Winkler heat scale from Region I to Regions II and III, and that classification, from D1 chapter 3, is the idea our next series is built on.

One worth arguing about. You are planting Pinot Noir in Anderson Valley: the foggy, frost-prone valley floor at Philo, or a ridge above the fog? Make your case.

**Open question (gate 4, settled by decision: the closing page is the "Cheers!" sign-off).** Two defensible positions. *The valley floor:* Region I at Philo, the cool end, with fog and a long season (D3: cold air funnels in along the Navarro; D1: cool sites keep acidity), at the price of frost risk in low ground. *The ridge:* above the fog, full sun, rarely any spring frost (TTB), at the price of steep timbered slopes, vineyard blocks rarely over 30 acres (TTB) and a tiny planted area. A WSET reader can argue either, which is what makes them answer.
