Read the dashboard first: dry, medium alcohol, medium (+) acidity and body. Then the notes. Brioche, hazelnut and pastry crust are what yeast gives a wine as it breaks down over time (autolysis), and the creamy mousse says what kind of wine this is. Together they narrow the field fast.

## PINNED COMMENT: post once guesses are in (the second beat)

Answer: Roederer Estate L'Ermitage Brut 2019, Anderson Valley, California. Roederer Estate is the California house of Champagne Louis Roederer. Wine Enthusiast gave it a 100, the first it has given an American sparkling wine.

A second look at page 2: the dosage is 6 g/L. In the EU, extra brut runs from 0 to 6 g/L of sugar and brut goes up to 12, each with a 3 g/L tolerance. Where would you put this wine?

### Optional second comment, a day later (D4)
Why seven months on the cork after disgorgement? The dosage sugar reacts with the compounds the yeast released as it broke down (the Maillard reaction), building toasty, roasted aromas, so producers hold the wine for a few months before release.

### Source trace
- Alcohol 12.9%, dosage 6 g/L, 4,850 cases, disgorged February 2024, seven months on the cork, first malolactic vintage: the producer's tech sheet (see `arc3/render_wad_roederer_lermitage.py`). No lees time is stated anywhere on it, so none is printed.
- "The first it has given an American sparkling wine": K&L's wording ("first 100-point score for a US sparkling wine from Wine Enthusiast"); the producer's press release (5 June 2025) says "first-ever perfect score for an American-made sparkling wine from a major wine publication".
- Sweetness bands: D4 p. 11 (extra brut 0-6 g/L, brut 0-12 g/L, "a tolerance of +/- 3g/L permitted for each of these bands, e.g. Brut can be up to 15 g/L"), which agrees with Regulation (EU) 2019/33, Annex III Part A. The limit is on the finished wine's sugar, of which the dosage is most of it, so the comment asks a question and does not call the wine extra brut.
- Autolysis descriptors: D4 (traditional method: "brioche and/or biscuit autolytic notes"), D1 (lees: dough, biscuit, toasted bread).
- The Pacific and the acidity (page 1): D3 (a few miles from the Pacific) and D4 s17.1 (cool ocean breezes and fog drawn inland along the Navarro; "enable growers to maintain high levels of acidity in grapes"); 4.2 miles measured.
- Seven months on the cork (optional second comment): D4 p. 11 (post-disgorgement Maillard reaction; wines held a few months before release).
- Roederer Estate as "the California house of Champagne Louis Roederer": D4 s17.1 and s17.3; the producer's sheet.
