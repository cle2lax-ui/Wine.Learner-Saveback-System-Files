## CAPTION: paste as-is

Two oceans, one wind, and some of the oldest vineyard soils on Earth. This is why South African wine tastes the way it does.

Sixteen slides. Start with a wine country at the edge of Cape Town, then the map of 32 districts. See how a cold current and a strong south-easter keep a sunny country fresh. Taste Chenin from old vines, Sauvignon Blanc and Chardonnay in their Cape styles, and Stellenbosch Cabernet and the Bordeaux blends, then see how one mountain, the Simonsberg, makes two different wards. Add a sweet wine that Napoleon is said to have ordered by the barrel. Then a whale in Walker Bay, and Cap Classique, the Cape's bottle-fermented sparkling wine. We end among 9,600 plant species: about 95% of Cape wine grows in the Cape Floral Kingdom.

For WSET Diploma and CSW candidates: the facts are from D3 chapter 30 and D4 chapter 20, with color from the Wines of South Africa Level 2 course.

**The "so what" on each slide.** 1 a map of Africa, South Africa in gold. 2 a great city beside the vines. 3 a district on the label means every grape comes from it. 4 sun-drenched and sea-cooled, so the wines are fresh. 5 some of the oldest soils on Earth, and each rock behaves differently. 6 a modern Cape only about 30 years old. 7 half the Cape's certified old vines are Chenin. 8 Sauvignon Blanc and Chardonnay each come in four Cape styles. 9 Cabernet leads the finest reds, and the finest reds are blends. 10 a ward is a tighter promise than a district: the two sides of the Simonsberg make two styles. 11 dry-farmed old vines give concentrated fruit. 12 the ocean takes over, and Elgin is the coldest. 13 a wine fit for Napoleon. 14 Champagne's method with the Cape's sun. 15 fresh, toasty and made for food. 16 9,600 plant species.

**Why slide 10's wards matter (the "so what").** Under Wine of Origin, a ward is defined by common soils, climate and ecology (D3), so a ward on a label is a tighter promise of place than a district. The Simonsberg shows it: the Stellenbosch ward on its south-west side is recognized for Cabernet and Cabernet blends (Kanonkop, Rustenberg); the Paarl ward on the other side sits higher, with a bigger day-night swing and a longer, marginally cooler ripening season, and its Chardonnay shows notable freshness (Glen Carlou, Plaisir de Merle). There are nine wards on the map: Stellenbosch has eight, and Simonsberg-Paarl belongs to Paarl.

**CONFLICTS between the sources, and what was done.**
- **Cap Classique volume and exports:** D4 says about 3.9 million bottles a year sold, nearly 70% exported; the WOSA course says about 10.5 million produced and 80% drunk in South Africa. Neither number is printed. Both say the UK is the top export market, and that is what slide 14 says. Worth asking WOSA or the CCPA which is current.
- **Elgin's altitude:** D3 250 to 500 m, WOSA 250 to 400 m. Not printed.
- **District and ward counts:** the WOSA course (2023) says 30 and 98; WOSA's website, the polygon file and D3's era say 32 and 102. The map slide shows 28 districts in its window and does not print a count.
- **Years of winemaking:** D3 "more than 370 years" (from 1652) is used; WOSA says "more than 360" (first wine, 1659).
