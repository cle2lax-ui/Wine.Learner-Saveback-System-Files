## 3. Entries 2-6
**2. Guess the Wine Region.** Page 1 names nothing: clues from D3 (about 34 S; False Bay about 10 km away; about 1,000 mm of rain; east-facing slopes; decomposed granite over sandstone; no Pinot Noir because it is too windy). Page 2: Constantia, the oldest wine ward (1685), Vin de Constance lost in the late 1800s and revived by Klein Constantia in the 1980s (a Muscat dessert wine). Alternates, if Steve prefers a drier answer: **Elgin** ("the coolest wine-growing area of South Africa"), **Hemel-en-Aarde** (Pinot Noir and Chardonnay, clay up to 55%), **Swartland** (old, unirrigated vines).

**3. Five Fascinating Facts About South African Wine** (cover plus five): (1) Pinotage did not exist before 1925; (2) Chenin Blanc covers double the Loire's area; (3) until the 1990s under a third of the grapes became wine, and by 2017 about 85% did; (4) the Cape Doctor cools the vineyards and cuts the yield; (5) Constantia's Vin de Constance vanished and returned. Reserves: the Swartland's old vines, Elgin as the coolest area, Cap Classique since 1971 (D4).

**4. Split Decision, "Pinotage: Alone or in a Blend?"** Side A: bottle it alone (a distinct South African grape; quality up with better vineyard and cellar practice, D3 p. 508). Side B: Cape blend (30-70% Pinotage with Bordeaux or Rhone varieties or Cinsaut, p. 511). Built to the Split Decision style guide.

**5. What Am I Drinking?** An old-vine Chenin Blanc from the Swartland or the Olifants River's Citrusdal Mountain (D3 pp. 516, 519: unirrigated old vines, ambient yeast, old oak, premium to super-premium). The page-1 clue must not name Chenin; the reveal ties back to the Field Guide. **Needs Steve's bottle photo and producer sheet** (send a 1,500 px or larger shot).

**6. Summary Reel** (optional). The Field Guide bumper, then the Cape in a handful of beats.

## 4. Rules for this arc
- **Facts: D3 first.** D4 for Cap Classique only. The CSW guide is a cross-check, and where it differs (older planting figures, the 1918 KWV date), D3 wins and the difference is logged.
- **Known D3 inconsistency:** Swartland rainfall is 400 mm in the climate section (p. 506) and "around 500 mm" in the district entry (p. 516). The deck prints "400 to 500 mm" or leaves it out.
- **History is stated plainly.** Slavery, the "dop" system and apartheid appear as D3 states them, in plain words, once, in the Field Guide's timeline slide. They are not decoration, a pun or a hook, and they are not in the Facts or Guess posts.
- **Producers:** only those D3 names (Klein Constantia, Hamilton-Russell, Sadie, Mullineux, Alheit, Kanonkop and so on), spelled as D3 spells them. No unsourced claims about a producer.
- **Photos:** location-verified Wikimedia Commons (probe category names first). Needs: Constantia, Stellenbosch with the mountains, Swartland bush vines, Elgin, Hemel-en-Aarde, a Franschhoek or Paarl view, old Chenin vines. Credits on each page.
- **Map:** Natural Earth coastline with the districts placed by latitude and longitude, labeled "schematic".
