A mountain wall, a very dry year and six years in barrel. Can you guess this red?

Swipe for the clues, then the answer. The mountain wall shelters these vineyards from the worst of the Atlantic weather, which makes the climate mostly continental. Climate sets the stage, but weather writes the vintage: this red comes from a year with 25% less rain than the average at the time.

## NOTES (not for the caption)

**Guess-format rules kept.** Nothing in the caption, hashtags included, names the wine, producer, vineyard, grape, region or country. Page 1's build guard also fails if any of 16 hidden terms (the producer, the wine, the region, the grapes, the river, the range) appear in its text.

**The climate tie, and where each piece comes from.**
- The mountain wall: D3 p. 345, the Sierra de Cantabria "protecting Rioja from the worst of the Atlantic weather". "Mostly continental, with some maritime influence": D3 p. 346, on Rioja Alta. D1 p. 43 supplies the concept: regions "protected from ocean influences have high continentality" and are classed as continental.
- "Climate sets the stage, but weather writes the vintage": D1 p. 44 (weather is the annual variation against the climatic average; vintage variation), with D3 p. 347 (in any year, Rioja gets more or less Atlantic influence, cooler and wetter, or Mediterranean influence, warmer and drier).
- "25% less rain than the average at the time": Wine Advocate (Luis Gutierrez), via Prima Vini's page, which gives the 2012 as "a very dry year". The qualifier "at the time" is Wine Advocate's own and is kept.
- "As the climate warms, Rioja's higher sites are more prized": D3 p. 347, climate change "has made high altitude vineyards not just more viable, but more desirable". It is the Field Guide's slide 15 ("new sites").
- A Reserva needs 36 months of aging, 12 in barrel: D3 p. 330. Six years in barrel: Wine Advocate / Prima Vini.

**Callback.** Slide 14 of The Field Guide (the Bordeaux 2013 vs 2016 comparison) is the same idea, weather against climate. Anyone who read it has the key; anyone who didn't still has the clues.

**Difficulty.** Medium to hard. The cover paragraph never says the grape, the country or the river, and the structure (high acidity, pronounced aroma, long finish, six years in barrel) points to an old-school Spanish red for a taster who knows the styles.

**Not used.** The 450 m altitude and 100 ha amphitheater (Prima Vini only; D3 gives no figure for Haro); the blend percentages (Prima Vini's 75/15/10 disagrees with Decanter's 70/20/10, so none are shown); D3's Rioja Alta soils (they do not describe Haro).
