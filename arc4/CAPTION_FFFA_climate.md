## CAPTION: paste as-is

Black pepper may disappear from Northern Rhône Syrah. Five facts on what a warming climate does to wine.

A warmer vine runs a faster year: budburst comes earlier and every stage quickens. Sugar and acid race ahead while aroma and tannin ripen on their own schedule, so grapes may be picked riper. Some vineyard sites in regions with severe heat and drought are thought likely to be abandoned within 50 to 100 years. And places once too cold to ripen grapes may gain, though erratic weather and altered rainfall still pose a risk.

For WSET Diploma and CSW candidates: all five come from the climate change section of D1 chapter 3, and The Field Guide: Climate's Impact on Wine covers the factors behind them (slide 15 is the short version).

One worth arguing about. Your vineyard is in a region facing severe heat and drought. Do you move uphill, change the grape, or hold on? Our next post takes a side.
