## NOTES (not for the caption)

**The answer is not in the caption.** "Willamette", "Oregon" and "California" appear nowhere, and none of the five hashtags names the place. `#pinotnoir` is already clue C. The hook says "American" so the quiz is "which region", not "which country" (the same move as Baden's "German").

**Clues, each checked against the source.** A: 15.9°C is D1 p. 30. B: D1 p. 30 says the west coast of North America, "including Oregon, is cooled by the cold California current, which flows from the northern Pacific"; the current is not named, because "California" would mislead. C: D1 p. 30, "the main grape in the Willamette is early-ripening Pinot Noir". D: D1 p. 30, both "at approximately 45° latitude"; it is the release valve (Margaux is the foil, never the answer).

**The hedge carries over from the Field Guide review.** D1 says Margaux's warmth is only "at least partially due to the Gulf Stream", so the Gulf Stream is not asserted anywhere. The 1.8°C gap is derived (17.7 minus 15.9) and stays off the slides; both of D1's own numbers are shown instead.

**Callback.** Slide 6 of The Field Guide (the Willamette vs Margaux duel) gives the answer away to anyone who remembers it; anyone who didn't has four clues that stand alone.

**Difficulty.** Medium. A knowledgeable solver gets it from "Pinot Noir + cold Pacific current"; clue D (Margaux, 45° N) is what makes it solvable for everyone else.

**Not used (true, but they place the region too easily):** 11,000 ha, 700 wineries, Portland, and the California Current by name. The Van Duzer Corridor wind (the best Willamette fact) is saved for a later post.

**Hashtags: five, to the cap.** Rotated from the Baden block (`#germanwine` out, `#winestudy` in); not a repeat of the Field Guide's block.
