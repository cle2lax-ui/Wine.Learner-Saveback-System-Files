### 2 — The Scale Problem (`editorial_lead`)
**Kicker:** NORTH VS SOUTH
**Headline:** One River, Two Wine Regions
**Standfirst:** The Rhône is treated as two regions separated by a 50 km gap where almost nothing is planted — and they have almost nothing in common.

- **North** — 4,200 ha, continental climate, one black grape.
- **South** — 65,000 ha, Mediterranean climate, blends built on Grenache Noir.
- **The gap** — 50 km (30 miles) with relatively few vines between them.

*Payload fact:* the northern crus account for just **6%** of the Rhône's AOC production by volume. Côtes du Rhône alone is 49%.

### 3 — The Map (`map_atlas.regional_atlas`)
Seven crus north to south: Côte-Rôtie · Condrieu (+ Château-Grillet) · Saint-Joseph · Crozes-Hermitage · Hermitage · Cornas · Saint-Péray.

### 4 — The Mistral (`spectrum`, photo bottom)
**Kicker:** THE WIND
**Headline:** The Wind That Makes the Wine Better and the Business Worse
The Mistral blows from the north. It suppresses fungal disease and it suppresses the vine — lower vigour, lower yields, **higher concentration, less wine to sell.** On the steepest slopes trellising is impossible, so individual vines are tied to one or two poles (*échalas*).

### 5 — Syrah, Alone (`duel`, columns)
**Kicker:** THE ONLY BLACK GRAPE
**Headline:** Seven Appellations, No Second Opinion
Syrah is the *only* black variety permitted in the northern Rhône crus.
- **In the glass:** deep ruby; medium to pronounced violet, plum, blackberry; black pepper and herbal notes; acidity and tannin both medium to high.
- **Red plum in cooler years and sites, black plum in warmer ones** — the same grape reporting its site back to you.
- **In the vineyard:** vigorous, needs tying in against the Mistral, susceptible to mites and botrytis bunch rot, plus *Syrah decline* — leaves redden, the graft union breaks up, the vine dies.

### 6 — The 20% Nobody Uses (`fact_file`)
**Headline:** Co-Fermentation, and the Rule That Mostly Goes Unused
Up to **20% Viognier** is permitted in some northern Rhône reds. In practice it is often **zero, and normally no more than 8%**. Where both are used they **must be co-fermented** — not blended after the fact. Viognier contributes floral lift and fruit.

### 7 — Côte-Rôtie (`side_rail`)
250 ha today. **70 ha in the early 1970s** — commercial interest had collapsed and the work was punishing. Revived by Etienne and Marcel Guigal's single-vineyard bottlings (La Mouline, La Landonne first) and the scores Robert Parker gave them.
East and south-east facing terraces · 10,000 vines/ha · max 40 hL/ha · mass selection, rootstock 3309 preferred.
**Style:** pronounced aromatics, softer and less full-bodied than Hermitage or Cornas.

---

### 8 — Hermitage (`side_rail`)
137 ha, virtually all planted, **one third of it white.** A south-facing slope on the left bank above Tain-l'Hermitage, named for Gaspard de Stérimberg, a twelfth-century crusader turned hermit. Thin stony soils, hot and dry; pronounced flavour intensity, high tannins, longevity. Le Méal, at the western end, records the highest temperatures.
**The philosophical split:** Chapoutier works individual parcels; Chave has long argued for blending across the whole appellation. Cave de Tain, the co-op, owns 15%.
In very ripe years, a rare **Vin de Paille.**

| Saint-Joseph | 50 km long · 40 hL/ha | Extended in 1994; now debating shrinking back to hillsides only |
| Crozes-Hermitage | 1,700 ha · 45 hL/ha | The largest; deeper, more fertile soils, lower concentration |
| Cornas | 145 ha · 40 hL/ha | South/east amphitheatre; usually the first Syrah picked in the north |
| Saint-Péray | 45 hL/ha | Whites only, Marsanne-dominant, on limestone and granite |

### 11 — Cellar Logic (`process_map`)
Syrah is **prone to reduction** — so it gets pumped over more often and is often aged in oak for gentle oxygenation, which costs money. (Grenache in the south has the opposite problem: prone to oxidation, so concrete and steel.)
Cru reds: hand-harvested in small crates · whole bunch or destemmed · warm ferment · maceration 20–30 days · 12–24 months maturation, small barrels for Syrah, typically 20–30% new.
