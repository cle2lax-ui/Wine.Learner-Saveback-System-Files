It's best known for red wine, but most of its vines are white. Name the German region.

Four clues on the first slide, answer on the second. It is Germany's warmest, sunniest region, it sits across the Rhine from Alsace, and an extinct volcano shapes its fullest wines.

If you swiped through our Mosel guide you have already met this region's number: 64 per cent of its wine is trocken, the tallest bar on slide 7.

Answer in the comments before you swipe. And once you have it: if you were handing someone their first glass from here, would it be the red or the white?

## NOTES

**Hook** is 86 characters, inside the fold, and front-loads the tension that defines the region (a reputation for one colour, a planting majority in the other) without naming it. It says "German" so the quiz is "which region", not "which country".

**The answer is not in the caption.** "Baden" appears nowhere, and neither does Kaiserstuhl or Spätburgunder (the reveal caption names the Kaiserstuhl; the caption must not). Neither `#baden` nor `#spatburgunder` is used. `#pinotnoir` and `#germanwine` narrow the field without giving it away.

**Close is genuinely open** — red or white as a first glass — which turns a quiz into a conversation and lets people argue for either colour. That serves the brief: the post is about a region where both matter.

**Callback.** The 64 per cent trocken figure is on slide 7 of the Mosel Field Guide, where Baden is the tallest bar. Anyone who swiped that deck has half the answer; anyone who didn't has four clues that are sufficient on their own.

**Difficulty.** Easier than the Saar: Baden is an Anbaugebiet and a Germany-warmest clue plus "across the Rhine from Alsace" narrows it quickly. Clue D (the volcano) is the one that separates people who know the region from people who deduced it. "Across the Rhine" matters: Pfalz also borders Alsace, but on the Rhine's west bank.

**Source trace (D3 Ch. 11, Aug 2026 ed.).** Warmest, sunniest; opposite Alsace on the eastern bank; Spätburgunder most planted; 61 per cent white; Kaiserstuhl an extinct volcano with the fullest-bodied wines; 64 per cent trocken in 2021. All are in the Baden section or the climate and variety passages. Nothing here is from outside the chapter.

**Cut from the first draft** (too long for the two-line clue limit, not wrong): the Grauburgunder/Weissburgunder clue, and Müller-Thurgau as the leading white. Also deliberately not used: co-operatives' ~75 per cent share, and Zone B enrichment, which would hand the answer to a specialist before the clues do.
