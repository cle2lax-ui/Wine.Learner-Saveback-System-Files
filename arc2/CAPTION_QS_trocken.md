## CAPTION — paste as-is

A German wine labelled trocken can legally carry more than twice the sugar you think it does.

The base threshold is 4 g/L residual sugar. But there is a derogation: up to 9 g/L is still trocken, as long as the sugar does not exceed the wine's total acidity by more than 2 g/L. Riesling holds high acidity even when fully ripe, so it clears that condition as a matter of routine.

Which makes trocken a measurement, not a promise. A high-acid Riesling will taste drier than a medium-acidity Müller-Thurgau holding exactly the same sugar — acid is doing the work the label takes credit for.

The regional split is the tell. In 2021 trocken was just under half of German production, 64 per cent in Baden, and 26 per cent in the Mosel. Warmer regions get ripeness enough to balance acid without sugar. The northern edge mostly doesn't, and mostly doesn't pretend to.
