## The open question (gate 4)
> Trollinger came from Tyrol and Lemberger from what is now Slovenia, yet 98 percent of Germany's Trollinger and about 92 percent of its
> Lemberger are planted in Württemberg. Does a grape become local through its origin, or through centuries of adaptation?

Two positions a knowledgeable person can defend. **Origin settles it:** neither is indigenous (Trollinger's German name is "Tirolinger", "of Tyrol";
Lemberger is Lower Styrian), so "local" is wrong. **Adaptation settles it:** centuries in one region, almost all planted there, and the
official wine institute itself uses "native" and "specialty." It passes the guide's test ("Should Les Saint-Georges be promoted?" is a question; "which do you prefer?" is bait): it has
no single right answer, and answering it demonstrates expertise. The same question can be put about Malbec in Argentina or Zinfandel in California.
