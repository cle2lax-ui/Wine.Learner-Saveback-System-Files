## CAPTION: paste as-is

Nine in ten German vines were white in 1980. Today nearly a third are red. Five facts on the reds behind that shift.

The change was driven in part by German drinkers: counting imported wine, Germans bought as much red as white in 2021.

For WSET Diploma and CSW candidates, the regional split is the part to hold on to. Spätburgunder (Pinot Noir) thrives in warm Baden, Dornfelder leads the reds in Rheinhessen and Pfalz, the Ahr is four in five red on steep slate, and Württemberg runs on Trollinger and Lemberger (Blaufränkisch).

Baden is the twist: despite its red reputation, 61 percent of its plantings are white, the clue that gave it away in our Guess the Region post.

One worth arguing about. Trollinger came from Tyrol and Lemberger from what is now Slovenia, yet 98 percent of Germany's Trollinger and about 92 percent of its Lemberger are planted in Württemberg. Does a grape become local through its origin, or through centuries of adaptation? Make your case in the comments.
